# Intraday microstructure: opening-drive continuation + weak-close overnight reversal

**Verdict: a qualified 1 bp pass, not a cost-robust TQQQ beater.**

On the 2016-01-04 -> 2023-12-29 development window, with next-open fills and
a 2% band, the base strategy earns **54.29% CAGR with a 45.90% max drawdown
and a 1.30 Sharpe at 1 bp under the mandated BIL flat asset**. Against the
bar (36.72% / 82.29% / 0.82) that is **+17.6 pp of CAGR and 36.4 pp less
drawdown**, and the drawdown is below the 60% target.

The qualification is cost. At 5 bp the BIL version falls to **16.66% CAGR**,
below the bar, because the BIL leg doubles the fill count. With a cash-flat
execution it still passes at 5 bp (**37.76% / 46.53% / 1.01**), but at 10 bp
it falls to 15.68%. A realistic retail execution is between 5 and 10 bp, so
the honest expectation net of realistic costs is below the bar.

All numbers are from `research/bt.py`, 1-minute marks, next-open fills, 2%
band, long-only weights summing to <= 1. `result.json` is generated by
`strategy.py`.

## 2. Exact rules

Universe: QQQ (calendar only), TQQQ (position), BIL (flat asset). One
decision per bar close, fill at the next bar's open. Half-days use their own
last bar. The 1-minute panel is truncated at 2023-12-29 before any statistic
is computed.

```
gap    = open(09:30) / prior session close - 1
r5     = close(09:34) / open(09:30) - 1
r30    = close(09:59) / open(09:30) - 1
dayret = close(15:58) / open(09:30) - 1

Day sleeve
  if gap > +0.25% and r5 > 0:   buy at the 09:35 open
  elif r30 > +0.50%:            buy at the 10:00 open
  trailing stop: exit at the next bar's open if a 1-minute close is
                 8% below the highest close since entry
  otherwise:                    exit at the 15:59 open

Night sleeve
  if dayret < -1.0%:            buy at the 15:59 open
                                 exit at the next 09:31 open
```

If the day sleeve is still on at 15:58 and the night signal is true, the
position is held through the last bar and overnight. Otherwise it exits at
the 15:59 open. A night position still on at 09:30 is exited at the 09:31
open; the day entry is at 09:35 or 10:00, so the 09:31-09:34 gap is flat.

The trailing stop uses closes only. It is a price-based risk overlay, not a
volatility or drawdown regime. No daily trend SMA, no volatility/drawdown
regime, no relative strength, no drawdown recovery.

### Signal counts

Over 2,012 sessions: 600 night signals, 478 gap/r5 entries, 706 r30 entries,
934 sessions with at least one day entry. 1,396 TQQQ round trips over eight
years (~175/year); median hold 6.4 hours, mean wall-clock 16.3 hours
including overnight and weekend gaps (the night sleeve is a 15:59 -> 09:31
hold counted as one trade).

## 3. Where the return comes from

Sleeve attribution at 1 bp (same stop and execution):

| Both sleeves (base) | **54.29% / 45.90% / 1.30** | **58.43% / 45.75% / 1.37** |
| Day sleeve only | 44.12% / 46.72% / 1.13 | 46.27% / 46.63% / 1.17 |
| Night sleeve only | 7.80% / 8.64% / 1.42 | 7.94% / 8.35% / 1.50 |

| r30 day only | 30.98% / 44.25% / 0.96 | 31.96% / 43.85% / 0.98 |

The **day sleeve is the primary edge** (44.1% BIL at 1 bp, above the bar on
its own). The night sleeve is a small, low-drawdown add-on (7.8% CAGR,
8.6% DD) that lifts the combined result by ~10 pp through compounding and
by shortening the flat periods after weak closes. It is not the main engine.

Per-trade statistics for the base BIL 1 bp run:

- 1,396 trades, mean **+0.245%**, median +0.343%, win rate **56.0%**,
  standard deviation 3.00%.
- Mean trade return by entry year: 2016 +0.17%, 2017 +0.23%, 2018 +0.18%,
  2019 +0.27%, 2020 +0.21%, 2021 +0.32%, 2022 +0.21%, 2023 +0.37%.
  Positive in every year. The per-trade edge is stable; the compounding is
  high because the strategy is in the market on 43.7% of minutes and the
  winners are allowed to run.

The max drawdown is **45.90%, 2020-02-28 -> 2020-03-24**, the COVID crash.
It is a drawdown, not a return-concentration year: the trailing stop cuts
the crash days and the strategy still makes +165.8% in 2020. The worst single
days are 2020-03-09 (**-20.0%**, a night-sleeve gap with no stop),
2018-10-26 (-10.1%), 2022-10-13 (-8.8%), 2022-09-13 (-8.7%), 2020-03-27
(-8.0%), and 2022-06-16 (-7.8%). The best are 2022-12-13 (+11.6%),
2020-11-04 (+9.7%), 2020-04-07 (+8.7%), 2022-10-18 (+7.9%), 2020-11-05
(+6.9%), and 2020-06-16 (+6.4%). The -20% day is the reason the drawdown is
45.9% rather than lower: the 8% trailing stop applies only to the day sleeve,
not to the night sleeve.

## 5. Cost sensitivity and the BIL problem

| Cost | BIL CAGR / DD | Cash CAGR / DD |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 bp (gross, diagnostic) | 65.46% / 45.52% | 64.06% / 45.56% |
| 1 bp | 54.29% / 45.90% | 58.43% / 45.75% |
| 5 bp | 16.66% / 51.36% | 37.76% / 46.53% |
| 10 bp | -17.75% / 83.76% | 15.68% / 51.51% |

Each 1 bp of slippage costs about **9.4 pp of BIL CAGR** and **5.2 pp of
cash CAGR** between 1 and 5 bp. The turnover is 55,312x of starting equity
under BIL and 32,645x under cash; the BIL leg roughly doubles the fill count
(5,581 vs 2,790) because every switch trades both assets.

This is the central execution finding for the family: **a high-turnover
intraday strategy that satisfies the BIL mandate at 1 bp does not survive
5 bp under BIL**, even though the same strategy survives 5 bp with cash
flat. The BIL yield (1.39% CAGR) is far too small to pay for the extra leg.
The cash-flat variant is the economically relevant execution for this
turnover; the BIL headline is the mandate-compliant one.

At 10 bp both variants fail. The gross per-trade edge is +0.245%; a cash
round trip costs ~20 bp at 10 bp, and a BIL round trip ~40 bp, so the edge is
gone. The strategy is a 1-5 bp strategy, not a 10 bp strategy.

## 8. Causality and integrity checks

- **Truncation test.** Building weights on a panel cut at 2020-03-12 11:07
  leaves every weight before the cut session **identical** to the full-panel
  weights (`max abs diff = 0.0`). The same test at earlier cut points in the
  exploratory work passed.
- **No full-sample normalization.** All thresholds are fixed ex ante; the
  session features use only bars up to the decision bar. The trailing stop
  uses the highest close since entry, not a future peak.
- **No look-ahead in the stop.** A 1-minute close below the trailing level
  is observed at the bar close and the exit is filled at the next bar's
  open. The stop is not assumed to fill at the stop price.
- **Session handling.** Sessions come from `ts.date`; half-days use their
  own last bar. The 15:58 exit is the second-to-last bar of a full session,
  so the 15:59 open fill is flat overnight.
- **Weights.** Finite, long-only, minimum 0, maximum row sum 1.0.
- **Data.** Adjusted 1-minute QQQ/TQQQ/BIL from `data/intraday-hf/`,
  regular session only, development window only. No 2024-2026 data was
  loaded or inspected.

## 9. Honest verdict

**The strategy clears the development bar at the 1 bp headline under both
BIL and cash, with a 45.9% max drawdown, and it is a genuine intraday
price-action result.** The day sleeve alone earns 44.1% at 1 bp BIL; the
signal placebo averages 12.8%; the per-trade edge is positive in every
calendar year; and the drawdown is well below the 60% target.

**It is not a robust net-of-cost TQQQ beater.** The pass depends on the cost
assumption:

1. **BIL fails at 5 bp (16.66%).** The mandated flat asset doubles the fill
   count and the extra leg costs more than the BIL yield. The cash variant
   passes 5 bp at 37.76%, but that is a deviation from the mandate.
2. **Both variants fail at 10 bp** (BIL -17.75%, cash 15.68%). The gross
   per-trade edge (+0.245%) cannot support a 10 bp round trip.
3. **The night sleeve has no stop and carries gap risk.** The worst day is
   2020-03-09 at -20.0%, a weak-close overnight position that gapped through
   the stop. The day-sleeve trailing stop cannot control an overnight gap;
   the night sleeve is a separate, unhedged tail.
4. **The best-20-days cut drops BIL to 33.9%**, below the bar. The right
   tail is concentrated.
5. **The best placebo draw (55.6%) is close to the base (58.4%)**, although
   the placebo mean (12.8%) and p95 (33.6%) are far below.

If the programme's standard is a 1 bp headline with a cash-flat execution
caveat, this is a pass and a new family result. If the standard is a 5 bp
BIL pass or a 10 bp pass, it is an honest failure. I would not present it as
an all-weather TQQQ replacement; it is a high-turnover, high-volatility
intraday momentum system that is strongest in high-dispersion years and
needs cheap execution.
