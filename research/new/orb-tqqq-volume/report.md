# TQQQ opening-range breakout with volume/ATR confirmation — result

**Verdict: FAILS the mandate's 36.72% CAGR bar.** On the 2016-01-04 ->
2023-12-29 development window, at 1 bp, the strategy earns **17.89% CAGR
with a 21.38% max drawdown and a 1.02 Sharpe**. That is a large
risk-adjusted improvement over TQQQ buy-and-hold (36.15% / 82.29% / 0.80),
but the CAGR is less than half the bar and is slightly *below* QQQ
buy-and-hold (18.91%). At a realistic 5 bp the CAGR falls to 11.33%.

The result is a **risk-adjusted** result, not a pass. The rest of this file
documents the eight-cell grid, the placebo and cut tests, and the ablations
that explain why the mechanism does not reach the bar.

All numbers are from `research/bt.py` with next-open fills, a 2% band, and
1-minute marks. `result.json` is generated by `strategy.py`; the robustness
numbers are in `_robust.json` and were produced by the scripts named below.

## 2. Exact rules (summary; full pre-registration in `HYPOTHESIS.md`)

1. **Opening range** 09:30-09:59 ET: `or_hi`, `or_lo`, `or_vol`. A session
   needs at least 10 one-minute bars in the range.
2. **Session filters** (all known by 10:00): gap up (`open > prior close`);
   opening volume ratio `or_vol / mean(prior 20 or_vol)` in [0.7, 1.6];
   `(or_hi - or_lo) <= 0.6 * ATR14`.
3. **Entry**: first 1-minute close after 10:00 above `or_hi`, at most 60
   minutes before the close. 100% TQQQ at that bar's close; fill next open.
4. **Stop**: `entry - 0.55 * ATR14`; checked on every 1-minute low; exit
   signal on the breach bar, fill next open. Stop is assumed before a target
   in the same minute.
5. **Exit**: stop, optional target, or the second-to-last bar of the session;
   the last-bar-open fill keeps the book flat overnight. Cash flat (BIL
   variant in §4.5). One trade per session, no re-entry.

No look-ahead: the range ends at 09:59, the gap uses the 09:30 open, the
volume baseline is the prior 20 sessions, ATR is the prior 14 sessions, and
every fill is at the next bar's open. The truncation test in §4.6 passes at
three mid-session cut points.

## 3. Why it fails: the overnight arithmetic and the late entry

### 3.1 TQQQ's return is mostly overnight

On the same 2,012 sessions:

| Component | Mean per day (arithmetic) | CAGR |
|---|---:|---:|
| TQQQ close-to-close | +0.2146% | **36.15%** |
| TQQQ open-to-close (intraday) | +0.1006% | **11.89%** |
| TQQQ close-to-open (overnight) | +0.1112% | **21.62%** |

About 64% of TQQQ's log return is earned overnight. A flat-overnight, 1x-long
strategy gives that up by construction. To reach 36.72% from an 11.89%
intraday base, the per-day geometric intraday edge would need to be about
**2.8x** the unconditional one (0.1245%/day vs 0.0447%/day), which is the
same as adding ~25 pp of annual return. The best conditional cells in the
screen are ~0.3-0.5% per trade on 30-60 trades/year, which compounds to
~15-25%, not 37%.

### 3.2 The breakout enters after most of the move

On the 572 selected days:

| Measurement | Mean |
|---|---:|
| Opening gap | +1.667% |
| Open-to-close | +1.292% |
| Open-to-breakout-entry (pre-entry move) | +1.060% |
| Breakout-entry-to-close | +0.227% |
| Realized trade return (with stop) | +0.271% |

The filters select good days: 75.2% of the 572 close above the open, versus
54.5% unconditionally. But the 30-minute wait means the entry is, on average,
1.06 pp above the open; only ~0.23 pp of the day's 1.29 pp move is left.
The ATR stop improves the realized mean slightly (0.227% -> 0.271%) by
cutting some of the days that would have closed lower, but it cannot recover
the pre-entry move.

A hindsight diagnostic on the same 572 days (using the 10:00 filters, so
**look-ahead and not a strategy**) shows the timing cost:

| Entry | Mean per trade | Joint CAGR over 8y |
|---|---:|---:|
| 09:31 | +1.244% | 137.7% |
| 10:00 | +0.920% | 88.9% |
| Actual ORB entry (median 10:15) | +0.271% | 19.6% |

This is the central diagnosis: the day selection has value, but the
opening-range breakout gives up ~0.97 pp per trade by entering late. The
36.7% bar would require a much earlier entry, which in turn would require
the day filters to be known at the open. The opening-volume and
opening-range-width filters are not.

### 4.3 Placebo tests (`robust.py`, 500 draws each, event-level)

| Placebo | Mean CAGR | 5th pct | 95th pct | Max | Core percentile | One-sided p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Random days, same entry-time mix, same stop | 2.29% | -8.54% | 14.02% | 27.66% | 98.6% | **0.014** |
| Same 572 days, random entry time 10:00-15:00 | **21.84%** | 16.03% | 27.57% | 30.73% | 26.0% | **0.740** |
| Random days, 10:00 entry, no stop | -0.62% | -13.64% | 12.44% | - | - | - |

Two clear readings:

- **The day selection is real.** Random days with the same holding/stop
  mechanics produce 2.29% on average; the actual days produce 19.6%
  (event-level, no costs). p = 0.014.
- **The breakout timing is not the edge.** On the same days, a random entry
  time between 10:00 and 15:00 produces 21.84% on average, *better* than the
  actual ORB entry (19.6%). The opening-range breakout does not beat entering
  at an arbitrary time on the same filtered days. The result is day
  selection plus a stop, not breakout timing.

This is the most important falsification in the report. It does not mean the
strategy is untradeable; it means the "opening-range breakout" label is not
where the return comes from.

Readings:

- The **ATR stop is load-bearing**: without it, CAGR falls 4.4 pp and DD
  rises 13 pp. A fixed 1.5% or 2% stop is much worse because it is too tight

- The **ORB entry beats a plain 10:00 entry on all gap-up days** (17.89% vs

  So the opening range is not useless; it avoids gap-up days that never make
  a new high. But it is not the source of the per-trade edge either
  (placebo 2).
- The **failed-breakdown reclaim is a dead end**, exactly as in the earlier
  QQQ rebuild: 3.20% at best, negative with sensible filters. The mandate
  asked to consider it; it is rejected.

| CAGR | 19.60% | 18.74% | 17.89% | 16.22% | 14.56% | 11.33% | 3.63% |

Each 1 bp of slippage costs **~1.7 pp of CAGR** because turnover is 1,914x of
starting equity. This is a high-turnover strategy (572 round trips in 8
years, two fills each). The 1 bp headline is the task convention, not a
realistic retail assumption; at 5 bp the CAGR (11.33%) is below QQQ
buy-and-hold (18.91%) and the Sharpe (0.70) is below TQQQ's 0.80, so even
the risk-adjusted advantage is a 1 bp result, not a robust one. The edge
before costs is 19.60%, still far below 36.72%, so costs are not the only
problem.

**BIL vs cash flat.** Holding BIL while flat adds two fills per round trip
(sell BIL to buy TQQQ, sell TQQQ to buy BIL) and doubles the fill count to
2,289:

| Flat asset | 1 bp CAGR / DD / Sharpe | 5 bp | 10 bp |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Cash (primary) | 17.89% / 21.38% / 1.02 | 11.33% / 25.54% / 0.70 | 3.63% / 42.54% / 0.29 |
| BIL | 17.81% / 21.20% / 1.01 | 5.05% / 39.77% / 0.37 | -8.98% / 67.65% / -0.44 |

At 1 bp the BIL yield almost exactly offsets the extra fills; at 5 bp and
10 bp BIL is much worse. Cash is the correct flat asset for this turnover.

### 4.6 Look-ahead, session and data checks

- **Truncation test.** Weights built on panels truncated at 2020-03-12
  11:07, 2022-09-13 10:41 and 2018-10-10 14:33 are **identical before the
  cut session** to the full-panel weights (`diag_audit.py`). Passed.
- **Session boundaries.** Sessions come from `ts.date`, not a fixed bar
  count. Half days use their own last bar; the EOD exit is the
  second-to-last bar of that session. The 13:00 half-day close is handled.
- **Warm-up.** The local 1-minute archive starts on 2016-01-04, so
  `bt.panel(warmup_days=60)` returns no pre-2016 bars. The first 14 sessions
  have no ATR14 and the first 20 have no 20-session volume baseline; they
  are cash. This is why 2016 starts slowly. It is a start-of-sample
  limitation, not a look-ahead.
- **Missing minutes.** The opening range uses bars stamped before 10:00 and
  requires at least 10 of them. The volume baseline requires all 20 prior
  sessions. No global "last bar = 389" assumptions.
- **No full-sample normalisation.** ATR and volume baselines are rolling and
  shifted; no statistic uses future bars.
- **Data.** Adjusted 1-minute QQQ/TQQQ/BIL from `data/intraday-hf/`, regular
  session only. No new downloads, no 2024+ data.

## 5. How this differs from the existing strategies

- **`research/agents/opening-range-breakout`** is the closest relative. It
  buys a 30-minute ORB and then **holds TQQQ overnight for weeks** behind a
  QQQ 165-day SMA; its report explicitly shows that the intraday-only
  variants fail (2-10% CAGR). This strategy is the intraday-only version
  with **no moving-average regime**, a volume/ATR confirmation, an ATR stop,
  and a strict flat-every-night exit. It reaches 17.89%, better than the old
  intraday variants but still nowhere near the bar.
- **`research/revisit/opening-range-breakout`** is a QQQ
  **failed-breakdown reclaim** (reversal), sized at 0.5% risk, intraday. This
  is a TQQQ **breakout continuation** at 100% weight; the reclaim is tested
  here as an ablation and loses (3.20% at best).
- **`research/revisit/volume-orderflow`** holds TQQQ for five sessions after
  a heavy-volume absorption day. This is a one-session intraday trade; the
  only shared idea is that volume matters, and here the volume filter is a
  band (too much volume is bad), not a threshold.
- **`research/agents2/volume-orderflow`, `agents2/intraday-momentum`,
  `revisit/volume-orderflow`**: different assets, signals and holding
  periods. No code, weights or parameters are reused.

The strategy is standalone: `strategy.py` builds its own weights from the
1-minute panel and the only shared code is the required `research/bt.py`
harness.

## 6. Honest verdict

**It does not beat TQQQ.** 17.89% CAGR vs 36.72% (brief) or 36.15% (harness)
is a clear failure on the mandate's primary criterion. The strategy does
produce a much lower drawdown (21.38% vs 82.29%), a better Sharpe (1.02 vs
0.80) and a better Calmar (0.84 vs 0.44), and its daily correlation to TQQQ
is only 0.34. Against QQQ the trade is roughly 1.0 pp less CAGR (17.89% vs
18.91%) for ~16 pp less drawdown (21.38% vs 37.30%) and a 0.12 higher
Sharpe. That is a modest, not a dramatic, risk-adjusted improvement. If the
programme values risk-adjusted return, this is a genuinely different return
stream; if it requires a TQQQ CAGR beater, it is not one.

The deeper finding is structural. A long-only, 1x, flat-overnight TQQQ
strategy gives up ~64% of TQQQ's log return (the overnight component) before
it starts. The unconditional intraday drift is 11.89%/yr. To clear 36.72%
the per-day geometric intraday edge would need to be ~2.8x the
unconditional one (0.1245%/day vs 0.0447%/day), i.e. ~25 pp more annual
return. The ORB's honest result is 17.9% (1.47x), and the best grid cell
19.0% (1.56x). No amount of parameter tuning within this mandate closes a
17-19 pp gap; the gap is the overnight drift, and capturing it requires
holding overnight, which the mandate forbids.

Three specific weaknesses would matter even if the bar were lower:

1. **The breakout timing is not the edge** (placebo 2: random entry times on
   the same days do better). The day selection and the stop do the work.
2. **The edge is concentrated in 2020 and 2022.** 2016-2019 is 2.27% CAGR;
   removing 2020 or 2022 cuts the full result to 11-12%.
3. **Costs are severe.** 1,914x turnover means each 1 bp costs ~1.7 pp of
   CAGR; at 5 bp the strategy is below QQQ buy-and-hold.

What would change the answer: (a) a tradeable filter known at 09:30 that
selects the same good days (the hindsight 09:31 entry is 137% CAGR, but the
opening-volume and range filters are not known then); (b) an overnight hold
(the old swing ORB, which is a different mandate and has its own 48% DD
problem); or (c) leverage above 1x (not allowed). Parameter tuning alone
will not.

I did not inspect 2024-2026, did not edit the website, existing strategies,
git or the audit, and did not commit or push.
